## Methods

The analysis begins by defining the firm’s transaction exposure: a €4,500,000 receivable due in one year. All outcomes will be evaluated in U.S. dollars. The forward rate of 1.0875 USD/EUR will serve as a benchmark for a fully hedged position.

Three hedge families will be modeled and compared. First, a forward contract will lock in the exchange rate, generating fixed USD proceeds and eliminating uncertainty. Second, a money market hedge will use interest rate parity by borrowing euros today, converting to USD at spot, investing domestically, and repaying the euro loan when the receivable is collected. This creates a synthetic forward rate. Third, a euro put option will provide downside protection while preserving upside potential, with a premium cost of $0.015 per euro.

An Excel model will conduct scenario and sensitivity analysis, calculating net USD proceeds, break-even exchange rates, and comparative risk-return tradeoffs to support the final recommendation.
